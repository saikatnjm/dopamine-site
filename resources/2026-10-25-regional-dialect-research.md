# Regional dialect research: Bangladesh (review copy)

**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Status: everything here is `needs_review`.** None of it is on the site.

This is a web research pass across 13 dialect areas, done to make the regional-language section
personal: "তোমার এলাকায় কী বলে?". Six research agents each covered one area. They copied spellings
exactly from sources they actually opened, recorded a source URL and a confidence level for every
entry, and were told never to invent or complete a word.

**How to use this:**
1. Skim the comparison table and the findings below.
2. Check entries in the regional sections, which come after the overview and are unedited.
3. Tell me which to approve, correct or reject.

Only approved entries go into scored games.

**Size:** about 750 sourced entries plus about 150 phrases and sentences.

## 3. Signature words, fun words and phrases (best picks for games and posters)

- **Signatures:**
  - Sylhet: কিতা, বাক্কা, মাত
  - Rangpur: বাহে, ক্যাংকা
  - Barishal: মুই, মোগো, মনু, আমনে
  - Noakhali: the প→হ shift (হানি, হোলা, হেনী)
  - Chattogram: গম = good, কিল্লাই
  - Old Dhaka: হালায়, আমি বি, মগর, আবে
  - Jashore: কনে, -গের, খাবানে
  - Mymensingh: হাছুন, কুনুকা
- **Surprising meanings:**
  - Sylhet: মেঘ = rain; বিষ = pain (also Panchagarh)
  - Rangpur: গুদাম = button; ফল = egg
  - Chattogram: গম = good; তরুনজা = pomelo
  - Narsingdi: কাইত্তান = rain lasting days
  - Mymensingh: রিস্কিন্ডি = joking; আছব্বা = amazing
  - Jashore: দোস্তনা = moonlight; ভুরুম্যাও = a cat's meow
  - Sundarbans: the tiger is called বড় মিঞা
- **Phrases for "same question, different regions":**
  - "কোনটে যাবে বাহে?" (Gaibandha)
  - "কোম্মে যাও আয়?" (Barishal)
  - "আন্নে কোনাই যাইবেন?" (Noakhali)
  - "কতি জাইবা?" (Rajshahi)
  - "কোবায় যাও?" (Khulna)
  - "তুমি কোথি যাইচ্ছো?" (Barendri)
  - "Tũi honde jor?" (Chattogram)
  - "তুই কোটে যাচ্ছু বারে?" (Joypurhat)

## 4. Review rules and cautions

- **Districts aren't dialects.** Habiganj and west Sunamganj are closer to Mymensingh than to Sylheti. Southern Cumilla sounds like Noakhali. Faridpur changes every 15–20 km. The map should show **dialect regions** with district tags.
- **Romanized and † forms:** where a source gives only Latin letters or IPA, the Bangla spelling needs your or a native speaker's check before it goes live.
- **Sources that copy each other:** the Barishal blog, MTNews24 and the Banaripara portal are one source; Chapainawabganj's Wikipedia list and a 2012 blog are one source.
- **Excluded on purpose (keep off a fun site):**
  - an expression tied to a specific caste
  - a quote from the 2024 killing of Abu Sayeed
  - ভাতার and মাগী (impolite or vulgar senses)
  - Dhakaiya Urdu (a separate language)
- **Unusable in these reports:** BD-Dialect dataset entries that only it supports (it has clear errors).
- **Licences to check before reusing bulk data:**
  - the ChatgaiyyaAlap and BD-Dialect datasets (Mendeley)
  - Gwynn's dictionary (online use with attribution)

  Individual word facts aren't copyrightable, but always cite the source.
- **Books that would settle most doubts (not online):** Bangla Academy *আঞ্চলিক ভাষার অভিধান* (only as an OCR scan), Mohsin's Barishal dictionary, Moshraf Hossain Bhuiyan's Kutti dictionary, and Nur Mohammad Rafiq's Chattogram dictionary.
- **Raw downloads:** portal text, PDFs, datasets and OCR files are in Claude's session scratchpad and are temporary. Ask if you want any copied into the project.

## 5. Signature words and features (sourced)

- **কনে / কোহানে** (where), Jashore — S14, S15, S17.
- **-গের plurals** (আমাগের, তাগের, ওগের) — S14, S7, S2.
- **-তি infinitives** (খাতি, যাতি, শুতি) — S14, S5.
- **-বানে future** (খাবানে, যাবানে, দিবানে) — S14, S3.
- **k/kh sounds become h** (টাহা, মুহি, ডাহে, দেহে) — S14, S12, S15.
- **ল/র become ন** (নাউ, নাল, নোম, নস, নাত) — S14, S15, S16.
- **ছাওয়াল** (boy), Khulna–Jashore — S1.
- **ম্যালা** (a lot) and **জাড়** (cold), Jashore — S17.
- **Chuadanga:** কুষ্টা (jute), কোইদমে (sweet pumpkin), কোইতোর (pigeon), ভা (rice) — S7.
- **Sundarbans "জঙ্গলা ভাষা" (Khulna):** the tiger is called বড় মিঞা, বড় শিয়াল, কাবলীওয়ালা, ভোঁতড়, বা'লেট, or just শিয়াল. "অমুক বনে মানুষ পড়েছে" means a tiger has killed someone there. In the forest people avoid saying "death"; even after a death they say "ভাল হয়েছে". A steamer is ধোঁয়াকল/ধুমাকল, and a monkey is জায়গীর or বৈকারী. All from S4 — good "fun word" material.

## 6. Conflicts, doubts, and what I could not verify

- **Chicken vs dog:** কুহুড়ো is unverified, and the similar-looking কুহরি means "the dog's" in Jashore sources (S15, S16).
- **মেহুর:** Shahidullah's dictionary gives it for Pabna, Faridpur and Noakhali, not Jashore. The only Jashore word for cat I found is বিলেই.
- **Standard words in the viral list:** হেঁসেল, মালসা, পায়রা, গাই, কুমড়ো, বিচালি and গেলাস are standard Bangla, not regional.
- **No source at all:** খোতে, নাঙ্গলা দাড়ি, আহা (as chula), গেলেস, and the exact spelling হানংসেল.
- **Dictionary scan quality (S18):** I worked from OCR text, so spellings may be off. Entries I marked "scan unclear" in the tables need checking against the page images. One district tag ("ক", probably Kushtia) is ambiguous, so I did not use any Kushtia attributions from the dictionary.
- **Bagerhat:** the ভাষা.com "Bagerhat" list (কয়াল, গ্যাড়া, চেলা) is the same as the Khulna Division government list, so the district label is doubtful. The Kalatala page's own examples are Barishal-influenced.
- **Kushtia and Meherpur:** the sources only say the speech is close to standard Bangla with a Nadia/Shantipur accent. I found no word list for Meherpur, and the only Kushtia lexical item is a dictionary sense (পোলা = boy) that I didn't put in the table. Narail and Jhenaidah have only a handful of words.
- **Dates:** the journal sources are from 1979–1992, so some words may now be old-fashioned.
- **Two items left out on purpose:** ভাতার (husband) is glossed as impolite in the source, and মাগী has a vulgar sense.
- **Blocked:** the ResearchGate full texts and bn.quora returned 403 errors. I read the same journal papers directly from the journal's own site instead.

## 4. Signature words

**Barishal**
- **First person মুই, মোর, মোরা, মোগো**: the most consistently cited marker (S1, S3, S10).
- **মনু / মোনু**: S3 says hearing মোনু marks someone as from Barishal.
- **আমনে** ("you", polite) (S1, S10).
- **-মু future and "আনে"**, as in যামু আনে (S1, S8).
- **Question words কোম্মে and ক্যা/কা** (S2, S4, S8).
- **Address terms দুদু, ন্যাবাই, দাদো** (S2, S4).
- **বোলে** ("is it true that…") and **কইলোম** (warning "mind you") (S2).
- **Sound pattern**: শ/স → হ (হাপ, হউর), final ক → হ (টাহা, দেহা), -এ্যা added to names (করিম্যা) (S1, S2, S11).
- **The famous list**: হব্রি, নাহোইল, ওশ্যা, আইত্না, উগোইর, খাডাল, ঠাডা (S5/S4).
- **Kobila** (wife), from Mohsin's dictionary (S9).

**Faridpur**
- **-তি verb forms**: খাতি, ধরতি, যাতি দেকছি, and progressives like ভাবতিছে, যাতিছে (F1).
- **-হেনে place words**: এইহেনে, সেইহেনে, ঐহেনে (F1, F2).
- **Future endings -বানে / -নে**: যাবানে, যাবেনে, অবেনানে, and আসপে (F1, F2).
- **আমাগের / আমাগে** ("our") (F1), against Barishal's মোগো.
- **ম্যালা** ("a lot") (F1).
- **বাহে** ("father") (F1).
- **মাইয়ে, ছাওয়াল / পুলা**: girl and boy (F1, F2).
- **কিরোম** ("what kind") (F1, F2).
- **ট্যায়া / টায়ার** (money) (F1), against টাহা elsewhere.
- **Faridpur Sadar forms** such as করিছি, রাখিছি, দেখিছি (F3).

## 6. Conflicts, doubts and gaps

**Conflicts between sources**
- **Faridpur "টাকা"**: টাহা (F3 government page, W1) against ট্যায়া / টায়ার (F1 academic paper). Both may be in use. Show both.
- **Faridpur "ছেলে"**: পোলা (Banglapedia, B1), ছাওয়াল (F2), পুলা / পোয়া (F1). B1 gives ছাওয়াল as Khulna/Jessore.
- **Faridpur future tense**: -বানে (F2) against -মু (F1 পারমু/দিমু; W1 খামু). Both appear.
- **Faridpur ভাত vs বাত**: F3 gives বাত; F1's sentences write ভাত.
- **Barishal পোয়া** (S7) is unsupported elsewhere. B1 gives পোয়া for Chittagong, and S4 uses পোলা.
- **থোতমা / থুতমা**: S1 says থুতনি (chin), S7 says মুখমন্ডল (face), S4's sentence glosses it as মুখ.
- **Spelling of রান্নাঘর**: ওশ্যা, ওসসা, অস্যাঘর, ওশ্যাঘর. Treat as one word with varying spelling.

**Regions are not uniform**
- F5 says there is no single regional speech across Faridpur.
- Haldar (via W2) puts western Faridpur (Rajbari) in a transitional Khulna–Jessore group.
- Chatterji (via W1) calls south Faridpur a border zone.
- S1 says parts of Madaripur, Shariatpur and Gopalganj speak Barishali.
- The Saltha (Faridpur) page says local speech resembles Khulna, Faridpur and Gopalganj and changes every 15–20 km.
- X5 says Shariatpur speech shows Bikrampuri (Dhaka-area) influence.

**Source quality**
- S5, S6 and S4's word list are one source. Only S4's sentences are independent.
- W1's comparison table is in a section Wikipedia flags as unsourced.
- Mohsin's dictionary (S9) is seen only in romanized excerpts.
- The Bonik Barta page (S8) was cut off before its word list.
- A Google result labelled "ফরিদপুর" (faridpur.pabna.gov.bd) is Faridpur upazila in Pabna district. I did not use it.

**Not verified for either area:** ব্যাঙ, বালিশ, ঘুম, জামাই, বন্ধু, পাগল, রক্ত, দাদা/দাদি, নানা/নানি, "কেমন আছ?", "ভাত খেয়েছ?".

# REGION REPORT: Chattogram, Noakhali, Cumilla

I collected about 50 entries for Chattogram, about 45 for Noakhali and about 35 for the Cumilla region. Most come from 22 sources I opened. The Cumilla region is much weaker: most entries there come from one blog or from romanized thesis transcriptions.

Things to know before you use this:
- **Copying:** spellings are copied from the sources unchanged. Romanized items stay romanized. In a few places a PDF scrambled the Bangla letters; those are marked.
- **BD-Dialect (S17):** this published dataset has obvious errors (for example, Chittagong আপনি = "তুনী", where three other sources say অনে, and "টাকা দাও" given as "টিফিন দে"). I only used its entries when another source agrees; otherwise they are marked low.
- **Wikipedia summaries:** a summarizing tool once added a word that wasn't on the page. So I checked every Wikipedia table entry against the page's own source text.
- **Not found online:** Bangla Academy's আঞ্চলিক ভাষার অভিধান (1965) is the key dictionary, and I could not find it online. A Chattogram dictionary (Nur Mohammad Rafiq) appears only as a citation inside another article.

## 7. Conflicts, doubts and what I could not verify

- **Spelling conflicts:**
  - Chattogram lemon: হঁজি vs কঁঅজি.
  - Taka: Chattogram টিঁয়া/টেঁয়া vs S17's টঁহা. Noakhali টিয়া vs S17's টেহা.
  - Noakhali chicken: কুড়া vs মুরগি.
  - Noakhali "five o'clock" sentence: different versions in S5 and S6.
  - Chattogram "where": হন্ডে / হডে / কডে.
  - Chattogram "girl": মাইয়া / মেইফোয়া / ফুঁরি.
- **Unreliable sources:**
  - **S17 errors:** it has clear mistakes (তুনী for "you", "টিফিন দে" for "give money", বঁই for "father"). Its stand-alone entries are marked low.
  - **S9 claims:** it says ãi comes from English "I" and পোয়া from Dravidian. These are the author's own claims and not reliable.
  - **A search result I did not use:** it said water is "fua" in Chittagong. I never opened that source, so I left it out.
- **Area boundaries are blurry:**
  - S14 says southern Cumilla (Chauddagram, Laksam) sounds like Noakhali, and Daudkandi, Titas, Meghna and Homna sound like Dhaka.
  - S5 says Nangalkot, Monohorganj and southern Chandpur (Faridganj, Hajiganj, Shahrasti) speak Noakhailla.
  - S8 says Haldar puts Chandpur in the Dhaka group.
  - So a single "Cumilla form" can be misleading, and I have nothing specific to Chandpur.
  - Cox's Bazar differs from the rest of Chattogram (S10, S13).
- **Not verified for any region:**
  - কেমন আছ (Noakhali and Cumilla), খেয়েছ (Cumilla), যাচ্ছি.

# REGION REPORT: Sylhet

I collected about 75 sourced word entries and about 25 phrases for Sylhet. Most come from one academic dictionary plus the Bengali and English Wikipedia articles. Two gaps matter most: I found no traditional word for রান্নাঘর, and only a few words have a Bangla-script source.

**Read before using:**
- **Habiganj and west Sunamganj aren't really Sylheti speech.** Bengali Wikipedia (citing Gopal Halder 1986 and a 2016 Banglanews24 report) says they sit closer to the Mymensingh-East / Brahmanbaria dialects. A Sylhet researcher quoted in that report says Sylhet and Moulvibazar share one pattern while Habiganj and Sunamganj differ. Most "signature" words below are Sylhet / Moulvibazar Sylheti.
- **Spelling.** Bangla script is quoted exactly where a source gives it. Otherwise I give the source's romanization or Sylheti Nagri script. Anything marked † is **my own letter-for-letter Bangla rendering, not from a source**, so check it before showing it to users.
- **Gwynn's spelling system** (my reading of his entries): ŧ = ত, t = ট, đ = দ, c = স-sound (চ/ছ), k/χ = খ, f = প/ফ. So *kiŧa* = কিতা and *mac* [mās] = মাছ.

## 5. Conflicts, doubts, and what I couldn't verify

**Not verified:**
- **রান্নাঘর:** Gwynn only has *kicin* (an English loan from London usage); *ranna* = cooking. No traditional Sylheti word found.
- **"ভাত খেয়েছ?"** in exactly that form: only "ŧumra kaico ni?" (have you all eaten?) was found.
- Bangla-script spellings for most dictionary-only words (everything marked †).
- Words specific to Moulvibazar or Sunamganj, beyond the one district sentence.

**Sources I couldn't use:**
- **Banglapedia:** I found no Banglapedia entry for Sylheti.
- **Amrita Rani Das's 2017 PhD thesis** (fedoa.unina.it): the PDF download kept timing out.
- **Omniglot:** returned 403.
- **Unicode document n2592:** I only saw a search snippet, so nothing from it is used.

**Conflicts and doubts:**
- Spelling disagreements: *murgi* vs *murug*; *lau* vs *khodu*; ebla/one (Gwynn) vs *ongkhu* (Wikivoyage only).
- Two forms for "long time no see" (বাক্কা দিন দেখছি না vs Oto buile na dekhlam).
- Gwynn's *bat* entry means "share/portion"; rice is under a different headword I didn't open. Rice (ভাত) is sourced from S1 instead.
- Wikivoyage calls "mor nam oilo" formal, but English Wikipedia lists *mor* as very familiar.
- The Wikipedia comparison table is uncited, and Wikivoyage is uncited community content.
- Gwynn records the London-diaspora variety, so it may differ from usage in Bangladesh.

## 5. Conflicts, doubts and gaps

1. **What বাহে means.** S1 glosses it as "বাবাহে, বাপুহে". S5 says it replaces "বাপুহে" and is used to address people. S21 and S22 use it as a name for the language. A North Bengal University thesis reportedly calls it the short form of "Babahe" and says it is not used between siblings, but its server returned 502, so I never opened it. A Quora post saying "brothers" was not opened either. Some search engines suggested unrelated meanings; I ignored those because no source supports them. A safe on-site gloss: "affectionate address (from 'বাবা হে / বাপু হে'); also a nickname for the Rangpuri dialect".
2. **আঙা vs আঙ্গা.** Rangpur আঙা = রাঙ "colour" (S1). Lalmonirhat আঙ্গা = জামা "shirt" (S3). Keep them as separate words.
3. **পশ্যন / পশ্যেন.** S1 says পশ্যন = পরিবেশ "environment"; S5 says পশ্যেন = খাবার পরিবেশন "serving food". S1's gloss may be cut short.
4. **ইয়াতে / ওতি.** S1 lists both as ওদিকে; one is probably a typo.
5. **S9 (Rajshahi portal) vs S16 (Wikipedia).** S9 says চ/ছ become স/জ in Rajshahi. S16 says Rajshahi and Chapainawabganj are exactly the districts where this does *not* happen. S9's own example (ছেলে → বেটা) doesn't show the sound change it claims, and its "মুই খাইমোঁ" looks Rangpuri. So S9 is rated low-medium.
6. **S5 is a reused Gaibandha text on a Nilphamari portal.** The Domar page talks about "জেলার উত্তরের উপজেলা সুন্দরগঞ্জ", Gobindaganj and Fulchhari, which are Gaibandha upazilas, and it shares its opening paragraph with the Gaibandha district page. Whether its words belong to Domar or Gaibandha is uncertain. Several other upazila pages are copy-paste templates with wrong geography: Porsha mentions Debidwar, and Gomostapur and Sherpur say they border Tripura. I excluded all of those.
7. **The Chapainawabganj list isn't independent.** The S17 Wikipedia list matches S18, a 2012 blog marked "collected from Facebook". A commenter there says many spellings are wrong (for example ঢুঁড়া not ডুঁইড়া, গোঁঢ়্যা not গৈইড়্যা), and the two sources spell several words differently. The best source would be the PhD thesis "চাঁপাইনবাবগঞ্জের উপভাষা" (মোসা. মনোয়ারা খাতুন, Rajshahi University, 2024), but only its catalogue entry is online.
8. **The BRWDS dataset (S24) is noisy.** For example it gives Rajshahi "আমি = আমাক" and "মিথ্যা = মুজাক", and many cells are romanised. I only used cells that another source confirms.
9. **Two items to keep off a fun site.**
   - "বেচিয়া খায়" / "বেচে খাওয়া" (S20, S6) is used for marrying off daughters. S6 ties it to a particular caste.
   - "হামার বেটাক মারলু ক্যান?" (S26) comes from the 2024 killing of student Abu Sayeed.
10. **The JNU paper (S19) needed converting.** Its Bangla is in the old SutonnyMJ font. I converted ÔKwZÕ to কতি and KwZRvBev to কতি জাইবা myself.
11. **I could not verify:**
    - No source gives "কোটে" for Rangpur itself; it is attested only for Bogura-influenced Joypurhat.
    - Kurigram has no district-specific words beyond its mention in S3.
    - Natore and Sirajganj vocabulary beyond one sentence each.
    - **No sourced regional form for these comparison words:** বিড়াল, মুরগি, ব্যাঙ, বালিশ, বৃষ্টি, ঝগড়া, পাগল, তাড়াতাড়ি, বন্ধু, বাবা/মা, দাদা/দাদি, নানা/নানি, বউ (except Panchagarh মাইয়া = স্ত্রী) and জামাই (except ঘর জামাই).
    - BRWDS gives the standard form unchanged for পানি, ভাত and সুন্দর.
    - **Sources I could not open:** Khaborer Kagoj's Rangpur dialect report (403), the NBU thesis (502), Academia.edu and ResearchGate Rajshahi papers (403), and the 1903 Linguistic Survey of India (the scanned text is unusable).

## 4. Signature words per area

- **Old Dhaka (Kutti):**
  - হালা / হালায় used harmlessly about anyone (S1, S3)
  - আবে / আব্বে as a filler (S3, S5)
  - আমি বি for "me too" (S1, S3)
  - মগর / লেকিন for "but" (S3, S6)
  - ক্যালা for "why" and কেমতে for "how" (S1)
  - কল্লা, কদ্দু (doubled consonants) (S3)
  - গাঁইয়া and ডেমচি (S1)
  - Kutti cab-driver humour: "ঘোড়ায় শুনলে হাসবে" (S6)
  - Nicknames from S7 (romanized): Kala Jabun, Ged ("vulture"), Futkior ("sparrow", a female matchmaker), Kauwa Thutithe ("crow's tongue", a complaining woman)
- **Narsingdi:** এফি / হেফি / কুফি; কাইত্তান (days-long rain); হুরু!; আমনেরা; নসোন্দি (local pronunciation of Narsingdi); the extra word "দো" in Monohardi ("গেছুইন দো") (S14).
- **Tangail:** দেওয়া (rain), গুয়া (betel nut), দামান (son-in-law), তফন (lungi), কয়চান (quarrel), ম্যালা (many), গেদা (boy). The puthi "আগেকার ম্যালা কথা" lists these (S16).
- **Manikganj:** ত্যাম্বা / এ্যাম্বা, হকাল, কাইয়্যা (S15).
- **Mymensingh:**
  - পুত (S13)
  - হাছুন (S12, S22, S23)
  - রাও করা (S22)
  - রিস্কিন্ডি, আছব্বা, গন্না (S21)
  - বাপের বেডা, চোপা করা, আইলসার ঠাহুর (S19)
  - Proverb: "হাওর জঙ্গল মইষের শিং, এই তিনে ময়মনসিং" (Zila Parishad Mymensingh site, https://www.zpmymensingh.org.bd/?page_id=2327)
- **Jamalpur:** dropping initial র: অক্ত, উটি, আইত (S21).
- **Kishoreganj (Ashtagram):** the secret register ছহুম / সুকুন: টাইঙ্গা, ফিদর, রোস্নাই (sun or light) (S25).

## 6. Conflicts, doubts, and what I could not verify

**Spelling conflicts:**
- **Kutti:** মগর (S1, S3, S4) vs মাগার (S2); হাছা (S1) vs হাচা (S2); সোন/হোন (S1) vs হুন/শুন (S2); পোলাপান vs পোলাপাইন.
- **Mymensingh:**
  - এখন = অংকা (S21) vs অহন (S22); S22 also says হগলে = এখন, while S23 glosses [hogle] as "just".
  - আইলসার ঠাহুর (S19) vs ডাহুর (S20).
  - দুক্কু means আঘাত (S22), ব্যথা (a Mymensingh blog), and ছোট (Tangail, S18).

**Where Tangail belongs:** the Mymensingh portal (S19) puts Tangail, Narsingdi and Kishoreganj in the "Mymensingh cultural region". The Tangail portal (S16) says north Tangail has a Mymensingh accent. Treat district boundaries as approximate.

**What "Dhakaiya" means in S12:** the column is not stated to be Kutti. S11 explicitly separates the "Dacca dialect" from Kutti.

**Weak or unglossed sources:**
- S11 forms are phonemic romanizations and have some text-extraction noise.
- S23 forms are IPA-style and have no Bangla script.
- S19, S24 and some S21 sentences have no glosses. I did not add meanings.

**Not found, with no usable source:**
- **Mymensingh/Dhaka forms** for রান্নাঘর, মুরগি (except Tangail কুড়কা), বালিশ, ব্যাঙ, নদী, পাগল, বন্ধু, বউ, দাদা/দাদি, নানা/নানি.
- **The phrases** "কেমন আছ" and "ভাত খেয়েছ" (the closest is Mymensingh "কাইচুন – কাইয়াম", S22, low).
- **Districts:** Munshiganj, Gazipur and Narayanganj vocabulary.
- **Key books I could not open:**
  - Kutti dictionary (Moshraf Hossain Bhuiyan)
  - Bangla Academy আঞ্চলিক ভাষার অভিধান
  - নরসিংদীর আঞ্চলিক শব্দকোষ
